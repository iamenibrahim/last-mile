| source alert | **8.48** |
| rendered output | **6.78** |
| rendered output, excluding locked entities | **6.31** |

- at or below grade 7.0: **68%** of alerts
- at or below grade 7.0 excluding locked entities: **79%**
- n = 80 alerts long enough to score
- scope: the English render

**Finding.** The brief assumed source alerts read at grade 11-13. On this corpus they do not: NWS already writes in short, hard-wrapped sentences with a controlled vocabulary, so the measured source grade is far lower. The reading-level gain is therefore real but modest, and the dominant barrier in the data is that the text is English-only, not that it is written above grade level. Reporting this rather than the assumed premise is the point of running the harness.

**Floor.** County, town and river names are locked entities and are polysyllabic by nature, so they put a floor under Flesch-Kincaid that no simplifier can lower without breaking the safety guarantee. The 'excluding_locked_entities' figures show the score for the prose we actually control.

Number and road corruptions are the columns that move. Without the lock

they cannot occur undetected, because a tampered sentinel is missing by

## What this system does not claim

- It does not originate alerts. It only transforms authoritative ones.
- A valid manifest proves faithful transformation of a fetch we attest to.
  It does **not** prove NWS signed anything: CAP products served over
  `api.weather.gov` are not individually signed in a way this system can
  verify end to end. The trust anchor is TLS plus our own signature.
- It does not integrate with IPAWS. That needs a COG agreement.
- It does not detect deepfakes or manipulated alerts.
- Target-language readability is not measured. Flesch-Kincaid is English-only.
